# Vocabulary audit

Every reader-facing term in this tool, checked against documents a buy-side
professional would accept: SEC filings, CFA Institute standards and curriculum
material, FDIC and World Bank statistical definitions, Nareit standards, and
named practitioner frameworks. A term that is only *plausible* is marked as such.

Ordered by how much damage the term does if a portfolio manager challenges it.
The first five are the ones to fix before any demo.

## Preset descriptions: written by a desk, or by a model?

`asset_researcher/config/mandates/presets.yaml`

**The vocabulary is right. The structure gives it away.**

The nouns are genuinely desk-literate, and none of them is a tell: "incremental
ROIC", "free cash flow conversion", "we size to conviction", "hedge sector
exposure", "40-60 positions across both books", "probability-weighted payoff",
"dividend safety and coverage", "covenant pressure". The short-side list is the
best writing in the repository — "deteriorating unit economics, aggressive
revenue recognition, inventory or receivables building faster than sales, and
management guidance that keeps slipping" is a real screen, in the order a real
analyst would run it. Nothing there needs cutting.

What gives it away is that all four presets have the identical rhetorical
skeleton, in the identical order:

1. a one-clause self-identification ("Long-only fundamental fund.",
   "Long/short equity, one to four quarter horizon", "Event-driven fund trading
   equities and options", "Income-oriented mandate with a multi-year horizon"),
2. a "We care most about / We care about / Priorities are" clause,
3. a closing sentence naming the exit trigger ("We exit when…", "…the things
   that make us sell").

Three of four also contain a sentence whose only job is to tell the tool what
to de-emphasise:

- "Catalysts matter more than terminal value to us."
- "Long-run terminal value is largely irrelevant to us."

These are the phrases to cut. No PM volunteers a statement about terminal value
when describing how he invests — it is a statement *about a DCF*, which means it
was written by someone who already knew the tool runs one, aimed at steering it.
A reviewer who spots that reads the whole box as prompt-engineering dressed as
a persona, which then taints the credible material around it. "Income-oriented
mandate" is the other small tell: a PM says "income mandate", or more likely
just describes the portfolio and never uses the word.

The fix is cheap and does not touch the vocabulary: delete the two
terminal-value sentences, let the four presets differ in length and shape, and
let one of them be two blunt sentences rather than five polished ones. Real
self-descriptions are uneven. These are not.

One structural note: the file's own header comment says the description is
"written the way a PM would actually describe themselves, not as a label". That
is the right standard. The presets currently miss it by being too uniform, not
by being wrong about the business.

## Could not source — cut or rename

These have no professional usage behind them. There is nothing to show a
reviewer who asks "where does that term come from?", so each one is a term that
can only be defended by argument, which is the position to avoid.

1. **"Counter-case"** — no usage anywhere. Use "Bear case" or "Risks to the thesis".
2. **"Variant view"** — the phrase does not exist professionally; "variant
   perception" does, and is attributable to Steinhardt and quotable from CFA
   Institute. One word apart, and only one of them is citable.
3. **"Prevailing view"** — the word is "consensus".
4. **"Trajectory"** as a section name — no research note uses it.
5. **"Market pricing"** as a section name — no research note uses it.
6. **"What must be true"** as a section name — traceable only to management
   consulting, not to equity research.
7. **"Health"** as a section name — the screens inside it are citable; the
   wrapper is not.
8. **"Note"** as a section name inside a note.
9. **"Questions"** as a bare section name — "Questions for management" is the practice.
10. **"Accrual ratio"** as a label — the concept is Sloan (1996) and is real, but
    there is no single ratio of that name, and the measure is academic rather
    than desk vocabulary. Label it by its arithmetic instead.
11. **"Additional sources"** — not a term of art in any research workflow.
12. **"Debt, long and short combined"**, **"FFO (as reported by the company, when
    tagged)"** — not wrong, but not language anyone outside this codebase would
    write. Internal naming that escaped into the interface.
13. **"Provenance"** as a *section heading* — the word is citable in research
    infrastructure (BlueMatrix's Research Provenance Standard), but not as
    something a PM reads in a note, and it duplicates "Sources".

**One item is not a vocabulary problem but a correctness problem, and it is the
most dangerous line in the audit:** `npl_ratio` is labelled "Nonperforming Loan
Ratio" and computed against total assets. The World Bank and IMF definition uses
total gross loans. The tool's own file applies the correct honesty rule to two
neighbouring metrics (`nim_on_assets`, `ffo_before_disposals`) and then breaks it
here. Fix the label before the demo.
